# Compression theory against WARP's actual bottleneck

A map of mathematical compression approaches, filtered by one criterion: does it
reduce **physical expert bytes read per accepted token** on a random-access NVMe
path, against the VQ3R baseline that already ships.

No new measurements. Lattice constants are from Conway & Sloane, *SPLAG*
Table 2.3. Format facts are from `docs/FORMAT.md` at `cb05b14`. Break-even
targets come from
[SPECULATIVE_DECODING_BYTE_ECONOMICS.md](../crossval/SPECULATIVE_DECODING_BYTE_ECONOMICS.md).

## The baseline that any proposal must beat

From `docs/FORMAT.md`:

- **VQ3R**: 3 stages x 256 entries, **dim 8**, residual VQ, 3.00 bits/weight
  exact (one byte of index per 8-dim vector per stage), plus one FP16 scale per
  output channel (16/n_in amortized).
- `W_expert ~= scale_per_channel * sum_{s=1..N} codebook_s[index_s]`
- Codebooks are **per-layer**, 256 entries, resident.
- The K3 expert record is **12,406,784 bytes = exactly 3029 pages**, one
  `pread`, 4 KiB-aligned. O_DIRECT depends on that alignment.
- Index layout is **blocked by 64 rows** to keep a tile's indices contiguous.

Three properties are load-bearing and constrain everything below:

1. **Fixed-width random access.** One expert = one aligned `pread`.
2. **Page alignment.** Losing it makes every read fail `EINVAL`, not merely run
   slower.
3. **Bounded decode arithmetic.** Q3G was killed because the scalar 3-bit unpack
   cost more in trunk matvecs than the cache saved in I/O — 1.4x slower *and*
   36% off in logits.

## The viability inequality

Any candidate must satisfy, end to end:

    (B_old - B_new) / NVMe_bandwidth  >  T_decode_new - T_decode_old

At the GB10's measured **5.919 GiB/s**, a 1% byte saving on a 26.459 GiB block
buys about **44.7 ms**. That is the entire arithmetic budget a new decode path
may spend before it becomes a loss. On a bandwidth-starved rig the budget is
larger; on this one it is tight.

## Sphere packing and lattice quantization

Normalized second moment `G`, and the granular gain over scalar quantization:

| lattice | G | gain (dB) | bits/dim | bytes at 3 bits/wt |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Z (scalar) | 0.083333 | 0.0000 | 0.0000 | — |
| D4 | 0.076603 | 0.3657 | 0.0607 | 2.02% |
| **E8** | 0.071682 | 0.6541 | 0.1086 | **3.62%** |
| Barnes-Wall L16 | 0.068200 | 0.8703 | 0.1446 | 4.82% |
| Leech L24 | 0.065770 | 1.0279 | 0.1707 | 5.69% |
| sphere bound (n->inf) | 0.058550 | 1.5329 | 0.2546 | 8.49% |

**E8 is dimension 8 — exactly VQ3R's vector dimension.** That is the one
structural coincidence worth noticing: an E8 lattice quantizer is a drop-in
replacement for a VQ stage at identical vector geometry and identical record
width, preserving page alignment and fixed-width access by construction.

### The honest caveat, stated first

**These gains are measured against *scalar* quantization, and VQ3R is not
scalar.** It is trained k-means residual VQ in dim 8, which already captures
much of the granular gain the table above attributes to lattices. The 3.62%
figure is therefore **an upper bound on what E8 could add, not a prediction**,
and the realistic increment over a well-fit codebook is likely a fraction of it.
Anyone quoting 3.62% as an expected saving over VQ3R is misreading the table.

What E8 offers that is *not* speculative is on the other side of the ledger:

- **Zero codebook bytes.** E8 is generated by a rule, so the resident per-layer
  256-entry codebooks disappear. On a 92-layer container that is real resident
  RAM returned to the expert cache.
- **Decode is arithmetic, not a gather.** `docs/FORMAT.md` notes VQ3R's gather
  is scalar because a 256-entry table exceeds `vqtbl4q`'s register file. E8
  decoding is a rounding rule plus a coset correction — SIMD-friendly, no table.
- **Fixed width, trivially.** Page alignment is preserved by construction.

### Ceiling check

Halving bytes at 3 bits/weight needs 1.5 bits/dim of gain. The sphere-packing
bound at infinite dimension is 0.2546 bits/dim. **Lattice packing alone cannot
approach a 2x byte reduction — it is short by roughly 6x.** Anyone proposing
lattices as a path to 2x is proposing something the theory forbids. They are a
*percentage* mechanism, which is exactly the right size for the 2.13%
speculative break-even and the wrong size for the 25x gap to a $3k rig.

### Cheapest falsifying experiment

Offline, no engine change: take one layer's expert matrices from the existing
container, fit VQ3R as shipped, and compare against an E8 quantizer at matched
bits/weight in the **activation-weighted** metric. Kill if E8 does not at least
match VQ3R's error while eliminating codebook residency. Under an hour on CPU.

## Rate-distortion: choosing the distortion, not the rate

The right frame is `min D(W, W_hat) s.t. R(W_hat) <= R_0`, and the whole
question is what `D` is.

`docs/FORMAT.md` contains a decisive worked example. The parked shared-low-rank
section reports, in the **unweighted weight metric**:

- pairwise overlap of rank-72 dominant subspaces: **0.046** vs random baseline
  **0.031** (identical = 1.0);
- shared basis captures **7.1%** of energy vs 3.1% random vs 20.0% own.

The document then states the reason not to delete it: those are unweighted
measurements, and *"every expert sees the same hidden-state distribution, and
LLM hidden states concentrate in a few dominant directions, so in the
activation-weighted metric the useful directions may be shared by construction
even though the weights are orthogonal here."*

That reasoning is correct and it generalizes past low-rank. **Weight-space
orthogonality is not evidence of output-space independence.** Experts are
near-orthogonal as matrices while their *outputs on the narrow manifold of real
hidden states* can share a low-dimensional subspace. Any bit-allocation result
derived in the unweighted metric — including negative ones — is measuring the
wrong distance.

Candidate distortions worth testing, in rough order of expected value:
activation-weighted error, output-logit error, route-frequency-weighted
allocation, per-layer sensitivity, then KL and greedy-continuation agreement as
acceptance gates rather than optimization targets.

### This is the highest-value item on the map

`docs/FORMAT.md` already preregisters the experiment and its revival condition:

> *"Rerun the Gate 3 comparison with an importance matrix from real activations
> ... Revive if a whitened shared basis buys >1.5 pp of error at <=0.15
> bits/weight; otherwise delete the section and the field."*

It is parked for want of a rented GPU session. **It does not need one.** An
importance matrix is a second-moment accumulation over real hidden states —
a CPU streaming job. We have that capability and an existing routing-trace
pipeline, and the preregistered threshold means the result is interpretable
whichever way it lands. We would like to run it and report both outcomes.

## Transform / incoherence methods

Blockwise Hadamard or randomized sign-rotation before quantization makes weight
blocks more isotropic, which is precisely the precondition under which lattice
quantizers realize their theoretical gain. QuIP-style incoherence processing is
the reference point.

This is the natural **partner** to E8 rather than a competitor: E8 assumes
roughly isotropic blocks, real expert weights are not, and a Hadamard transform
is O(n log n) with a cheap exact inverse and no effect on record width or
alignment. Test only after E8-vs-VQ3R at matched bits, and only if E8's shortfall
looks like anisotropy.

## Block-local entropy coding

Global entropy coding is **disqualified**, not merely risky: variable-length
streams destroy the one-`pread`-per-expert property and the 3029-page alignment
that makes O_DIRECT legal. A conforming design would need independently
decodable blocks, a small offset index, bounded scratch, no cross-expert
dependency, and a fixed maximum read amplification.

Note the trap the VQ4P result already demonstrates: it was **complete, exact,
and still a loss** — 7.192 vs 7.081 tok/s, reading **8.11% more expert bytes**
despite equal nominal bits, because it followed a different quantized
trajectory. Nominal compression ratio is not physical bytes read. Any entropy
scheme must be measured on trajectory-inclusive physical reads.

| approach | expected | vs 2.13% break-even | verdict |

| E8 lattice replacing a VQ stage | <=3.62%, likely less | marginal | test second; codebook removal may matter more than the packing gain |

## What this map concludes

**No compression mechanism on it reaches a 25x gap.** Lattice packing is bounded
at 8.49% by the sphere bound; the honest expectation over VQ3R is low single
digits. These are percentage mechanisms.

That is not a reason to drop them — it is a reason to aim them correctly. A
2.13% verifier byte reduction flips speculative decoding from +1.91% to a net
win, and several items here plausibly clear 2.13%. Large multiples, if they
exist, live in *residency and reuse* — the 34.2% union sublinearity that
currently converts to only 3.69% of avoided reads — not in the encoding of
individual weights.

## Gates before any implementation

Source/layer/block provenance; on-disk bytes **including** codebooks, indices,
and metadata; physical bytes read per generated token; decompression cycles and
scratch; cache hit/miss and eviction impact; logit KL and route divergence;
greedy continuation agreement; wall time and energy; fixed-width or bounded
block random access; **and comparison against VQ3R, not against FP8 or dense**.

Byte and quality ceilings get preregistered before implementation, and negative
results get published with the ceiling intact — the practice that made the
FreeToken prefill gate and the VQ4P negative useful to us.
